Papers

A Pre-Registered Protocol for Testing a Local Intraday Regime-Break Candidate in the S&P/TSX Composite
A. K. Touré, S. Mishra, D. Shah · Preprint, Sep 2026
DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22706824

If you search a day of prices for the best-looking break, you will find one even when nothing happened. We fixed the test windows in advance, used block-bootstrap nulls, and controlled error across all 24 candidates with Romano–Wolf / max-T. In our Monte Carlo stress test (AR(1), intraday seasonality, Student-t shocks) the familywise size came out at 3.98% for a 5% target.

What I mean by alpha

If you're a quant

r − r_f = α + β·(factors) + ε

  • Escalator: β is the ride, α is the walking. Alpha is the intercept of a factor regression, so it only exists relative to a model. Add size, value and momentum and some of the walking turns out to be a faster escalator.
  • Lemonade: compare with the right benchmark on the same day, never with zero.
  • Coin kids: the best of N backtests is biased upward by selection. Deflated Sharpe ratio, a t > 3 hurdle, or a max-T bootstrap fix this. My regime-break paper is built on it.
  • Noise: real alpha is still hard to see. With an information ratio of 0.5 you need about 16 years of returns to get t = 2.
  • Fishing spot: alpha decays as it gets crowded and shrinks as you trade bigger. Only alpha after spread, impact and fees counts.
